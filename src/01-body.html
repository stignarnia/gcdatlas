controls

Every frame is drawn only with printable ASCII characters. Denser glyphs mean more light; colour comes from the physics (blackbody temperature, Doppler shift, emission lines).

drag
orbit the camera
scroll / pinch / + -
zoom, all the way from a moon to the observable universe
scale ladder (right)
drag the marker to zoom; let go near a name, or click one, to travel there (on a phone: tap the scale chip at the top right)
ruler (top left)
at the distance of the object you are looking at, the bar's length equals the distance written beside it
click or tap
fly to an object and lock onto it
two fingers
on a touch screen: pinch to zoom; slide both fingers to move the object across the screen (the camera stays on it); double-tap the sky to centre it again
/ or the search box
search everything in the atlas by name or by catalogue number (M31, NGC 1976); arrow keys and Enter pick a result
right-drag / shift-drag
pan: the camera lets go of the object (free camera); play, space or the "back to" pill flies back to it
next stop · Moon ›
the green button beside the name: on a tour, fly on to the next stop ("start again" on the last one; on the random tour, "new random tour" picks 12 new places); after you leave a tour, "back to the tour" picks it up where you left it
GRAND TOUR ▾
the tour's name above the object's name opens the list of tours
[ ]
on a tour: previous / next stop; otherwise down / up the scale bar (Moon, Earth, Jupiter, the Sun, ...)
‹ angle 1/5 ›
the arrows on the angle line: previous / next camera angle of the object in view (every tap moves one angle)
W A S D · R F
fly freely, within reach of the nearest object (scroll out to go further)
space · ❚❚ / ▶︎
pause or play: the tour, or the angles of an object you picked (they loop until you take the camera); in free camera, back to the object you left

esc

Everything sits at its real position and real size: the planets where they are today, 1,982 naked-eye stars at their measured distances, the Milky Way, its neighbours and the cosmic web out to the edge of the observable universe. Brightness is compressed so faint things stay visible, stars twinkle gently for life, and fast processes (orbits, explosions, mergers) are sped up. The few objects too small to see next to their surroundings (a quasar's engine, a kilonova) are drawn magnified, and the ruler shows their true size. The music is generated in your browser as you listen. The Halo, a long-range cruiser that wanders between wonders, and Pip, its little drone, are the only invented things here. The Sun is drawn warm yellow-orange, like a filtered solar photo; from space its light is white.

This needs WebGL 2, which this browser or device has turned off.
